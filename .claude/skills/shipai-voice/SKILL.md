# Ship AI — Tone of Voice

Ship AI is free, practical AI education in Phoenix — workshops on the tools and workflows builders use day to day, with every deck, guide and skill published free. The voice is a builder teaching builders: direct, concrete, allergic to hype.

**Headline (Sep 2026):** "Practical AI, Taught Free." It replaced "Demos over memos" as the lead line because the sessions are now mostly educational, not live building. Lead with what people learn and take home; keep "ship" as the verb for what they do with it.

## Core rules

1. **Practical over theoretical.** Talk about the tool, the workflow, the thing someone can use Monday morning — never "thought leadership". Concrete nouns beat abstractions: name the model, the tool, the workflow. What people take home (the deck, the guide, the skill) is the proof.
2. **Craft over hype.** Never use marketing superlatives ("game-changing", "revolutionary", "🚀 to the moon"). The most excited the voice gets is *"this is worth your Saturday."*
   - **Pitches are welcome — as demos.** Founders showing their product is on-brand; a founder demo *is* a demo. The line is **hard selling**, not pitching: no closing, no "buy now", no lead capture, no lead lists. Frame it as "show the product, skip the hard sell" — never "no pitches" (that wrongly excludes founders).
3. **Receipts required.** Claims come with specifics. Not "we go deep" — "20-minute news briefing, then the workshop, then 5-minute demos."
4. **Proof of work.** Show it and prove it. Claims are cheap - numbers on screen, a live URL, the thing running. This is the standard we hold ourselves to before we ask it of anyone else.
5. **Honest starting points.** Say where you actually are. We can't help you from a position you're pretending to be in - "I have no customers yet" gets you further here than a fake traction slide.
6. **Community-driven framing.** "You" and "we" — this is a room of builders, not an audience. Never talk down or gatekeep ("must be technical to attend" → "follow along or just watch").
   - **Reassurance is not the pitch.** "Nobody gets put on the spot" is a true, useful detail for someone already deciding whether to walk in — keep it in venue/format copy like a group description. Leave it out of anything meant to excite: leading with what *won't* happen to you is a defensive frame, and it makes the room sound like a thing to survive rather than a thing to be part of.
7. **The reader is the hero. Ship AI is the guide.** (Santos, Jul 2026 — the single biggest lever on copy quality.) They've already done the hard part; they have the skills, the repo, the working thing. What they're missing is the other half nobody taught them, and a room to do it in. So: open on *their* situation, not our program. Name the problem as circumstance, never as their failing. Hand them a plan and a first step. We supply the map, the room, and the receipts — they supply the courage and the work.
   - Hero: "You've built the thing. It works. It's sitting in a repo nobody's ever seen."
   - Not hero: "Ship AI is running a six-session program on go-to-market." (True, and it makes us the protagonist.)
   - The energy this unlocks is *inspirational*, not hype — the excitement comes from what they're about to do, so it never needs a superlative to carry it.
8. **The verb is "ship", not "launch".** (Santos, Jul 2026.) "Ship" is the brand's word — it's in the name, the mantras ("just ship it", "if it ships, it speaks"), and it's what builders actually say. "Launch" is marketing-department vocabulary and it reads borrowed. So: *ship your product*, *the weekend you ship it*, *nobody wins for building, you win for shipping*.
   - **The one exception is the proper noun.** The program is named **Zero to Launch** and the site, all six event titles, and every platform description use it. Don't rename it mid-program and don't "fix" it in a title. The rule governs the *verb*, not the name.
   - Watch for the ones that hide: "launching", "a launch", "pre-launch", "launch day", "ready to launch". Rewrite each as a form of ship.
9. **Short sentences. Active voice. Sentence case.** No exclamation-point stacking. One exclamation max per post, usually zero.
10. **Plain hyphens in copy that goes through browser automation** (Discord, Meetup, LinkedIn paste pipelines) — literal em-dashes can get mangled. Use `-` when in doubt.
11. **Phoenix only.** Never Tempe, never Chandler — Ship AI dropped Tempe as an operating city; don't reference it in new copy.
12. **Never state a relative timeframe you haven't computed.** "this week", "tomorrow", "this Wednesday", "coming up next week" must be checked against *today's actual date* and the event date before they ship — count the days. A spotlight for an event 20 days out that says "this week" is the kind of error that reads as careless. When unsure, prefer an absolute reference ("Wed Aug 5") or a computed phrase ("about three weeks out") over a vague relative one. Today's date is in the session context; if it isn't obvious, run `date`.

## Two voices: the community and the studio

This skill is the **community** voice (shipai.club: free workshops, programs, the hackathon). The paid build arm, **Ship AI Studio** (shipaistudio.com), has its own voice in `studio/docs/brand-voice.md`: a senior operator talking to an operator who answers for the money. Plain, specific, calm, careful; numbers before adjectives; the control named next to the outcome. Studio copy never uses the community mantras on sales pages.

Rules both voices share (Santos, Oct 2026):

- **No hedging.** No "Example" labels or captions on sample screens, no "this one is an example", no footer disclaimers like "example data is illustrative" or "employer names describe the founder's career". Fictional sample companies already read as samples. Never claim a real client or result.
- **Lead with the firm, not the founder.** The founder's career belongs on About and in one supporting line. Lead with what the brand does differently.
- **Visuals are brand voice too.** See `shipai-brand` for the Star fix visual system.

## Event title house style

2-4 words. No emoji, no colon-subtitle, no ampersand feature lists. The specs go in the description, not the title.

- Yes: `GTM Engineering` · `Zero to $3,000` · `Outbound Agents` · `Ship the Surface`
- No: `Socratic Night ⚡ Your Topics, Live Debates & 5-Min Demos` (the retired pattern - emoji, subtitle, feature list, all three)

## Vocabulary

- Yes: builders, craftspeople, ship, demo, workflow, toolchain, receipts, high-signal, bleeding edge, session, workshop, Zero to Launch
- No: ninjas/rockstars/gurus, synergy, "AI-powered future", disrupt, "don't miss out", FOMO framing, "limited spots" (unless literally true), **"launch" as a verb** (see core rule 8 — the program name Zero to Launch is the one exception)
- Mantras (use sparingly, one at a time, as closers): "practical AI, taught free", "proof of work", "receipts required", "just ship it", "feel the fear and do it anyways"

## Formatting for readability (event/announcement posts)

Santos wants social posts scannable, not wall-of-text and not a dry listing. Standard structure:

1. **Hook line** — a values/why opener, not logistics ("Most talks tell you how it's done. This one gets built on screen while you watch." / "Shipping code every day and launched nothing - that's the problem this program exists to solve.").
2. **Format line(s)** — what actually happens, often as `→` steps: briefing → the workshop → 5-min demos.
3. **Labeled detail block** with visual anchors (these genuinely aid scanning and are on-brand — Santos's own Meetup copy uses them):
   - 🗓 date · time
   - 📍 venue - address
   - 🔗 RSVP link
4. Close with a mantra when it fits ("practical AI, taught free" / "just ship it").

Emoji: restrained and functional (anchors 🗓 📍 🔗 💬 🎤 →), never decorative spam. One hook, one detail block, one link. Blank lines between blocks. Discord can use `**bold**` headers; X and LinkedIn use line breaks + anchors (no markdown renders there).

## Post-event recap register (Santos, Aug 2026)

Recaps (the thank-you + resources post after a session) run in a **more professional register than announcements**: polished, full sentences, with at most a slight edge. Santos rejected a punchier draft as "way too edgy" — the staccato-fragment-mantra style that works for announcements reads wrong when you're thanking people.

Rules, in addition to the core ones:

1. **Open with thanks, in a complete sentence.** "Thank you to everyone who joined us for [event] at [venue] on [day]" — not "X is in the books" or any fragment-lede.
2. **No mantras in recaps.** Save the mantras ("just ship it" / "feel the fear") for announcements and the room. The closest a recap gets is a factual flourish.
3. **One edge line maximum, and it's factual, not attitude.** The approved shape: *"a B2C case study, taught with the analytics open"* / *"taught from the actual dashboard."* The edge is radical transparency stated plainly — never slang, never a boast.
4. **Complete sentences throughout.** "The complete recording (with captions), the deck, and photos from the evening are now available, free:" — subject, verb, object.
5. **Structure:** thanks → what was covered (one sentence, recap posts only) → resources block (🎥 recording 🖥 deck 🛠 repo, each a full URL) → next session with date/time/venue in prose → sponsor thanks → `[ ATTACH 2-4 PHOTOS ]` marker.
6. **Sponsor thanks mirrors the recording outro's ordering** (venue host first, then community partners, then paying sponsors as listed in the outro card) so every surface credits identically.
7. The Meetup email variant is the same register but warmer — it's addressed to people who were in the room, can use the 🗓 📍 🔗 anchors for the next-event block, includes an `[ ADD 3-4 PHOTOS HERE ]` section, and may close with one mantra and a signature ("- Santos"), since email is the most personal of the brand surfaces.
